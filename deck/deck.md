## 2 / 15 — The problem

# India grows the most storied pepper in the world and exports almost none of it

The 2024/25 crop fell to a record-low ~48,000 t while domestic consumption ran ~81,000 t. India net-imported pepper.

What does leave the country leaves as anonymous bulk. A buyer in Rotterdam cannot prove the bag they bought came from Kerala at all — and the market's default answer to that is cheaper pepper from somewhere else, re-bagged.

---

## 3 / 15 — The wedge

# Verified provenance at lot level is the scarce input — not pepper

Growers sell ungraded at ₹620/kg. Specialty retail sells at ₹2,400/kg.

That is a 3.9× ladder. It is not climbed by being better at freight. It is climbed by owning the grade, the test panel, and the mark.

**B2B — 25 kg kitchen-grade micro-lots.**
MG-1 to restaurant groups and meal-kit kitchens who need a named origin and a printed test panel, not a commodity quote.

Every SKU carries a KL-YYYY-NNN lot number. No number, no sale.

---

## 5 / 15 — How it works

# The lot gate

A lot cannot be sold until all of this is true:

1. Signed supplier declaration: no ethylene-oxide fumigation
2. Moisture and water activity measured at intake
3. Full panel — mycotoxins, microbiology, heavy metals, pesticide multi-residue, EtO
4. Grade verified by sieve and density

Only then does it get a lot number.

This is the whole company in four lines. Everything else is logistics.

## 10 / 15 — Compliance

# The four numbers that decide whether a shipment lives

**Aflatoxin B1** 5.0 µg/kg max in the EU · **total aflatoxins** 10.0 µg/kg
**Ochratoxin A** 15 µg/kg for pepper
**Salmonella** absent in 25 g
**Ethylene oxide** — banned for food use in the EU, measured with 2-chloroethanol, effectively zero tolerance

We test every lot in India before it ships. The US has no EtO tolerance for spices either. There is no market where a positive result is negotiable.

## 11 / 15 — Moat

# Why this is defensible and not just trading

- **Lot-level chain of custody** with named grower clusters — expensive to build, boring to copy
- **Rejection data looped back to clusters**, so our supply improves and the spot market's does not
- **A brand that prints its own test results**, which is a liability for anyone selling on price alone
- **Two channels on one lot gate** — D2C carries margin, B2B carries volume predictability

A trader can undercut our price. A trader cannot undercut our paperwork without doing the work.

## 14 / 15 — Risks

# What actually kills this company

**Ethylene oxide contamination in incoming lots** — Contract clause with every supplier: no EtO fumigation. Test every lot before it leaves India. Steam treatment only. Reject-on-positive with cost recovery.

**Aflatoxin and ochratoxin A above EU limit** — Moisture spec max 12% at intake, covered drying, per-lot mycotoxin panel, reject lots from suppliers with two failures in a season.

**Salmonella detection** — Validated steam treatment, post-treatment re-test, sealed liners, no re-bagging in open air.

**Origin fraud — cheaper foreign pepper re-bagged as Indian** — Lot-level chain of custody, named grower clusters, isotope and trace-element screening on a sampling basis, published lot numbers.

## 15 / 15 — Why now

# Zero duty, a depleted crop, and an under-built premium story

- Whole pepper enters the EU and the US at **0% duty**
- India's FY 2024-25 spice exports hit a record $4.72 B, but pepper is only ~3% of that basket by value
- The 2024/25 crop collapse pushed domestic prices up and made provenance a buying criterion, not a nice-to-have

Buyers with budget have started asking for the test panel. Most suppliers cannot produce one on demand.
