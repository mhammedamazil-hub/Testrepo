# Kollam Pepper Co

### Single-origin Tellicherry pepper, cleaned and graded in Kerala, shipped from the Malabar coast under our own mark.

**The problem.** India grows the world's most storied pepper and exports almost none of it.
The 2024/25 crop fell to a record-low ~48,000 t against ~81,000 t of domestic consumption, so
the country net-imported. What genuine Indian-origin pepper does leave the country leaves as
anonymous bulk, and no buyer can prove where a bag came from.

**The company.** We buy from named grower clusters in the Western Ghats, grade to Tellicherry
and Malabar Garbled specification, test every lot to the destination's EtO, mycotoxin,
microbiological, and heavy-metal limits, and sell it under our own mark — retail packs D2C and
25 kg kitchen-grade micro-lots to restaurant groups.

**Why we win.** The scarce input is verified provenance, not pepper. Our moat is the lot-level
paper trail and the grading, which is boring, expensive, and exactly what the market does not do.

**The price ladder.** Farm gate ₹620/kg.
Specialty retail ₹2,400/kg.
A 3.9× spread, captured by owning the brand and the
documentation rather than by being cheaper at sea freight.

**Unit economics.**
B2B micro-lot: $320 revenue, $106 gross profit,
**33.1%** margin.
D2C pack: 37.8% contribution after CAC.

**Plan.**

Bear case on FY3: $537,120 revenue at 40.4% GM →
-$101,532 EBITDA. That is the number to survive.

**The ask.** $150,000 to fund two container cycles, the full compliance and
testing stack, a brand and a storefront, three trade shows, and a 90-day paid acquisition test
with a hard cut rule.

**Why now.** Duty is 0% into the EU and the US on whole pepper. India's export basket is 3%
pepper by value, which means the premium-origin story is under-built, not saturated. And the
buyers who care about a lot number are the ones with the budget.

**What we will not do.** Buy volume we cannot document, sell a lot before its panel clears, or
add fixed headcount before FY3 route density exists.
