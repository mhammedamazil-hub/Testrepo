## 1. What this company is

A Kerala pepper house that owns the grade, the testing, and the story — instead of exporting anonymous bulk to whoever bids last.

We buy whole black pepper from named grower clusters in the Western Ghats, grade it to
Tellicherry and Malabar Garbled specifications, test every lot against the destination's
contaminant and microbiological limits, and sell it two ways:

1. **D2C** — retail-ready packs sold under our own mark at specialty-food prices.
2. **B2B** — 25 kg kitchen-grade micro-lots to restaurant groups and meal-kit kitchens that
   need a named origin and a test panel, not an anonymous commodity bag.

We are not a trading house. We do not sell volume we cannot document.

## 2. Why this company, why here, why now

### The wedge

India grows the world's most storied pepper and exports very little of it. The 2024/25 crop fell to a record-low ~48,000 t while domestic consumption ran ~81,000 t — so India net-imported. Buyers who want genuine Indian-origin pepper pay a premium for it, and they cannot tell where any given bag came from.

### The insight

The scarce input is not pepper — it is verified Indian provenance at lot level. Our moat is the paper trail plus the grading, not the commodity.

### The strategy

Buy small, cherry-pick the top-grade fraction of Kerala lots, steam-treat and test each lot to destination spec, then sell retail-ready packs and B2B micro-lots under our own mark. Never trade volume for its own sake.

### The price ladder we are climbing

| Tier | Buyer | ₹/kg | Confidence |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Tier 0 — Farm gate / local trader | Farmer sells to a village aggregator; ungraded, no testing | 620 | high |
| Tier 1 — Regional processor / bulker | Cleans, grades, bulks in 25 kg bags, sells FOB on spot | 760 | medium |
| Tier 2 — Exporter / importer landed | Importer into EU/US pays CIF; duty is 0% on whole pepper into both | 1,000 | medium |
| Tier 3 — Retail / specialty brand | Buyer pays shelf price for 100 g - 1 kg branded packs | 2,400 | high |

The farm-gate to retail multiple is **3.9×**. Nobody in the
chain is capturing it by being slightly better at logistics. It is captured by owning a brand
and a verified origin, which is a paperwork and grading problem, not a shipping problem.

### Market facts we are allowed to quote

- India exported 17.99 lakh tonnes of spices and spice products worth USD 4,722.65 M in FY 2024-25, a record — *Spices Board of India via PIB, 2025*
- Black pepper exports rose from 17,890 t (FY 2023-24) to 20,830 t (FY 2024-25), value up from Rs 736.5 crore to Rs 1,055 crore — *Spices Board data reported Jun 2025*
- The FY 2024/25 Indian pepper crop fell to ~48,000 t on El Nino heat while domestic consumption was ~81,000 t — *Nedspice data reported 2026*
- Pepper is only ~3% of India's spice export basket by value — the premium-origin story is under-commercialised — *Spices Board 2026*

## 7. Compliance — the part that kills spice companies

The ECC `customs-trade-compliance` skill classifies goods by General Rules of Interpretation
in strict order. Pepper of the genus *Piper*, neither crushed nor ground, sits in heading 0904,
subheading 11 — GRI 1 resolves it on the terms of the heading, no GRI 2-6 analysis needed.
Crushed or ground pepper moves to 0904 12, which is a different line with different treatment
obligations. **Our product is never allowed to be sold as "pepper" without the form stated**,
because the form is what determines the classification and therefore the test panel.

### India — export stack

| Requirement | Authority | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| IEC (Import Export Code) | DGFT | Mandatory for any export |
| GST registration | GSTN | Mandatory and IEC-linked |
| FSSAI licence | FSSAI | Mandatory for any food business incl. export |
| CRES (Certificate of Registration as Exporter of Spices) | Spices Board of India | No scheduled spice may be exported without it; treated as RCMC under FTP 2023 |
| Phytosanitary certificate | PQMS / PPQS | Destination requirement for plant products |
| Certificate of Origin | Chamber of commerce | Needed for preferential claims and buyer files |
| RoDTEP claim | Customs | Refund of embedded taxes on exports; file per shipping bill |

### Destination limits

#### EU — 0904 11 00

MFN duty **0%**. Destination VAT applies on customs value plus duty

- Aflatoxin B1 max 5.0 ug/kg; total aflatoxins max 10.0 ug/kg (Reg (EU) 2023/915)
- Ochratoxin A max 15 ug/kg for pepper
- Salmonella absent in 25 g (Reg (EC) 2073/2005)
- Ethylene oxide and 2-chloroethanol: measured as sum, at or near the limit of quantification — treat as zero tolerance
- Lead max 0.6 mg/kg for fruit spices

> Pepper of heading 0904 from India is subject to a pesticide-residue check frequency at EU border control posts; confirm current Annex I frequency before each campaign

#### US — 0904.11.00.20

General duty **Free**.

- FDA registration and prior notice for every shipment
- Foreign Supplier Verification Program (FSVP) obligations sit on the US importer — we supply the hazard analysis and verification records to make them workable
- No US tolerance exists for EtO residues on spices; a detectable residue is a serious problem, not a threshold to negotiate

#### UK — 0904 11 00

Duty **0%**. UK-EU and UK-India arrangements change; confirm origin rules before claiming preference

**Zero duty does not mean zero risk.** Duty is the smallest line in the landed cost. The
aflatoxin, ochratoxin, Salmonella, and ethylene-oxide lines are the ones that destroy
inventory and, in the EtO case, are effectively zero-tolerance in the EU whether or not a
number is printed.

### Quality specification we hold ourselves to

| Parameter | Limit |
|---|---|
| moisture pct max | 12 |
| total ash pct max | 7 |
| acid insoluble ash pct max | 1.5 |
| bulk density g per l min | 500 |
| piperine pct range | 4.0-7.0 depending on grade |
| volatile oil ml per 100g min | 2 |
| foreign matter pct max | 0.5 |
| packaging | Food-grade liner inside multi-wall paper or kraft with valve; nitrogen flush optional |

### The lot gate

A lot does not receive a KL-YYYY-NNN number — and therefore cannot be sold — until all of
this is true. This is the ECC `quality-nonconformance` NCR disposition logic applied *before*
the goods are ours rather than after a customer complains:

1. Supplier declaration of no EtO fumigation, signed
2. Moisture and water activity measured at intake
3. Full panel: mycotoxins, microbiology, heavy metals, pesticide multi-residue, EtO
4. Grade verification by sieve and density
5. Only then does the lot receive a KL-YYYY-NNN lot number and enter sellable stock

## 8. Operations

### 8.1 The container cycle

One 20 ft container, 19 t net, 760 bags of 25 kg. Freight Nhava Sheva to Antwerp
$4,650.

| Stage | Days | Owner | Gate |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Grower lot offer and sample | 0 | Sourcing | Sample matches grade sheet |
| Intake test panel (per lot) | 7-10 | Sourcing + lab | All limits cleared, EtO not detected |
| Steam treatment | 3 | Processor | Post-treatment micro re-test |
| Re-grade and reject | 1 | Sourcing | 6% rejection allowance consumed or released |
| Export docs (invoice, packing list, COO, phyto, CRES) | 3 | Commercial | Buyer confirms spec in writing |
| Port, customs, RoDTEP filing | 4 | Commercial | Shipping bill accepted |
| Ocean transit | 24-30 | Carrier | — |
| Import clearance | 3-7 | Buyer | Destination release |

**Cycle time is 45-60 days, not 30.** Any plan that assumes the cash comes back in a month is
wrong by half.

### 8.2 Exception handling

The ECC `logistics-exception-management` skill puts roughly 40% of all exceptions into
"delay (transit)", and identifies customs hold and consignee reschedule as the two that hit
spice. Our severity rule is the skill's three-axis rule, compressed:

- Financial impact **and** customer impact **and** time sensitivity are each scored; the
  highest wins.
- Any exception that risks a customer's production line or a retail launch is **automatic
  Level 4**, regardless of dollar value.
- **Under $500 with a strong carrier relationship: absorb it.** Claims processing costs more
  than the loss and pollutes the carrier scorecard with noise. Log it, do not litigate it.
- **$500-$2,500: file, do not escalate.** Accept anything above 70% of value.
- **Over $10,000: VP-level, independent inspection, reject below 90%.**

Claims windows that matter: US domestic surface 9 months (49 USC § 14706), air 14 days for
damage and 21 for delay (Montreal Convention), concealed damage 5 days from delivery. We put
these dates in the calendar at the moment of booking, not at the moment of loss.

### 8.3 Inventory discipline

The ECC `inventory-demand-planning` skill says intermittent, lumpy demand should be forecast
with Croston or SBA, not a moving average. Our B2B desk is exactly that pattern — a restaurant
group reorders in bursts, not weekly.

- **Do not run safety stock on the B2B line.** Run a forward contract instead: commitment from
  the customer, commitment to the grower cluster, 90-day maximum inventory.
- **Run ABC classification on the D2C line only.** TGSEB is AX (high value, predictable
  seasonality). The gift box is BZ. If a SKU hits 26 weeks of supply and its last 13 weeks are
  below half of its first 13, it gets a markdown and a hard exit date.
- **No speculative stock on a falling crop.** The 2024/25 crop fell to ~48,000 t; India
  net-imported. Buying inventory on a price rally is how spice traders die.

## 11. Risk register

| Risk | Severity | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Ethylene oxide contamination in incoming lots | critical | Contract clause with every supplier: no EtO fumigation. Test every lot before it leaves India. Steam treatment only. Reject-on-positive with cost recovery. |
| Aflatoxin and ochratoxin A above EU limit | high | Moisture spec max 12% at intake, covered drying, per-lot mycotoxin panel, reject lots from suppliers with two failures in a season. |
| Salmonella detection | high | Validated steam treatment, post-treatment re-test, sealed liners, no re-bagging in open air. |
| Origin fraud — cheaper foreign pepper re-bagged as Indian | high | Lot-level chain of custody, named grower clusters, isotope and trace-element screening on a sampling basis, published lot numbers. |
| Crop and price volatility | medium | Forward contracts with two grower clusters, inventory never above 90 days, price-adjust B2B quotes every 30 days, no fixed-price retail commitments longer than 6 months. |
| D2C acquisition cost creep | medium | Hard 90-day cut rule on any channel above USD 18 CAC; shift budget to B2B where repeat is contractual. |
| Single-origin supply concentration | medium | Two sourcing districts minimum, plus a standing second source for MG-1 outside the premium line. |

### The one risk not in the table

**Counterparty risk on the grower side.** We are asking smallholder clusters to commit volume
before we have shipped anything. If we reject their lot, we own the relationship damage. The
control is not contractual, it is operational: pay for the lot at intake, test before we
accept, and publish the rejection reasons back to the cluster so the fix is visible. Suppliers
who never learn why they were rejected stop improving, and then they sell to someone who does
not test.
